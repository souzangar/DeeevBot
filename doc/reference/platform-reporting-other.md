# Platform reporting: other platforms

The owner's brief names Instagram, X, Facebook, TikTok and "possible other
platforms"; Telegram was added because the owner's own cases live there.

(`platform-reporting-instagram.md`, `platform-reporting-x.md`,

## How to report: what held on every platform read

The research of 2026-10-28 read Instagram, X, Facebook, TikTok, Telegram,
the EU Digital Services Act and the CSAM hotlines. Across all of them:

- Every platform has an in-app Report path on the post and on the profile
  or account, with a reason list, and most have web forms for people
  without an account (Instagram and Facebook: ask a friend or use a form;
  X: "An X account is not needed to report potential violations" for child
  safety; TikTok: web forms; Telegram: email).
- Reports are anonymous toward the reported account where the platform
  says anything (Instagram: "your report is anonymous, except if you're
  reporting an intellectual property infringement"; TikTok: "TikTok will
  not disclose your identity to the person you report."). Help Center forms
  can be an exception (X: "you may be asked to allow us to share parts of
  your report with third parties, such as the affected account").
- Child sexual exploitation: every platform says report it in-app and
  never download, save or share it (Instagram: "Never download or save this
  content for any reason."; X: "do not like, reply to, quote-post,
  screenshot, follow accounts who distribute, or otherwise engage with the
  content"; TikTok: "Never download, record, or share this kind of
  content."). The platforms report to NCMEC themselves. In this project the
  category is never broadcast; see [csam-hotlines.md](./csam-hotlines.md).
- Private information: Instagram and X may not act on a third party's
  report (Instagram: "Photos or videos involving anyone else will need to
  be reported by the individual themselves."; X: "we need a first person
  report" for private media and "may need to hear directly from the owner"
  for private information not "shared in a clearly abusive way"); Facebook
  cannot act for a child aged 13 to 17 on a parent's request. Expect the
  same on an unread platform until shown otherwise [INF].
- Bullying and harassment: a reason exists everywhere except in Telegram's
  static list; X and TikTok may want to hear from the targeted person or
  see persistence.
- Impersonation: a reason exists everywhere ("pretending to be someone",
  "Fake Account", "Pretending to Be Someone"); forms for the impersonated
  person sometimes require identification (TikTok's U.S. form).

## Policy pages: the EU Digital Services Act baseline

For members reporting from the EU, the DSA sets the floor on every platform
that is an online platform under it. EUR-Lex text [R]
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065:

- Article 16(2): a notice should contain "a sufficiently substantiated
  explanation of the reasons", "the exact URL or URLs", "the name and email
  address of the individual or entity submitting the notice, except in the
  case of information considered to involve one of the offences referred to
  in Articles 3 to 7 of Directive 2011/93/EU" (the child sexual abuse
  offences), and "a statement confirming the bona fide belief" that the
  notice is accurate and complete.
- Article 22(1): trusted-flagger notices "are given priority and are
  processed and decided upon without undue delay." See
  [trusted-flagger-programmes.md](./trusted-flagger-programmes.md).
- Article 23(2): "Providers of online platforms shall suspend, for a
  reasonable period of time and after having issued a prior warning, the
  processing of notices and complaints ... by individuals or entities or by
  complainants that frequently submit notices or complaints that are
  manifestly unfounded." Article 23(3) lists what platforms weigh: "the
  absolute numbers ... submitted within a given time frame", "the relative
  proportion thereof", "the gravity of the misuses" and "the intention".
- [INF] The DSA notice route needs substantiation and, except for the
  child-abuse offences, a name and an email; TikTok rejects thin
  illegal-content reports; Telegram's EU form needs name, contact details
  and an explanation. The broadcast's in-app path is lighter than the DSA
  route and is the default; the DSA route is for members in the EU who
  want to use it.

## Posture on volume, duplicates and false reports

Summary of the research note's cross-platform table (details with quotes
on each platform page):

| Platform | Duplicates deduplicated? | Reporter sanctions documented? |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Only for EU DSA notices ("applying the original manual decision"); in-app undocumented | Terms: "fraudulent or groundless reports"; Inauthentic Behavior standard; DSA Article 23(2) |
| Facebook | Same DSA sentence; in-app: "The number of times something is reported doesn't determine whether or not it's removed from Facebook." | Terms: "fraudulent, duplicative, or groundless reports"; account suspension possible |
| X | Not documented; bulk duplicates prohibited ("duplicate or false reports in large numbers, including repeatedly reporting the same post(s) or accounts") | Generic enforcement ladder; 0 DSA suspensions for unfounded notices in H1 2026 |
| TikTok | Not documented | EEA terms: "clearly unfounded" reports prohibited; thin illegal-content reports rejected |
| Telegram | Not documented | EU DSA guidance only; reports can automatically restrict the reported account |
| CSAM hotlines | Discouraged: "Please submit each report only once." (INHOPE); "Do report only once for each web address or URL." (ICMEC/IWF) | Not documented |

Reading notes that apply to any platform:

- No platform read documents that report volume raises priority or changes
  the outcome; Facebook says it does not. Meta's documented review
  prioritisation lists "SEVERITY", "VIRALITY", "LIKELIHOOD OF VIOLATING";
  TikTok's systems "may send posts with a high number of views to our
  moderators".
- Documented priority exists only for designated channels (EU DSA trusted
  flaggers, Meta Trusted Partners, TikTok's Community Partner Channel,
  YouTube's Priority Flagger programme).
- Good-faith, accurate reports are expressly allowed (X: "manually
  submitting reports in good faith that you believe may violate our
  policies"). The prohibited pattern everywhere is false, groundless or
  "clearly unfounded" reporting; Facebook (duplicative) and X (duplicate in
  large numbers) also name duplicates.
- The words "brigading", "report bombing" and "mass reporting" appear on
  none of the policy pages, terms, reports or help articles fetched. No
  platform text read mentions identical report wording as such.
- [INF] A broadcast asking every subscriber to file the same report is not
  forbidden by any text read, but on X it could fall under "coordinating
  and/or encouraging others to misuse X reporting features" if the reports
  are not genuine, good-faith reports; and nothing documented makes it
  faster. Hence the project's rule: one genuine report per member, in their
  own words, never a text to paste, never a promise of removal.

## What the bot's instructions say

For a platform without a reference page, the post quotes a fixed block
per category (the catalogs' `instructions.other.<slug>` keys), and a
report of the category "other" on any platform quotes the fixed generic
block (`instructions.generic`): since the scope reduction of 2026-10-28
no admin writes instructions, and the recommended comment is the only
admin text. The blocks name nothing platform-specific. They were written
from this checklist, which also governs any change to them, in English
(the Persian copy follows the same rules):

1. Name the in-app path you verified in the isolated browser profile: where
   the Report option is on a post and on a profile. If you could not find
   it, say "use the platform's Report option on the post and on the
   account" and nothing more specific.
2. Name the reason to choose only if you saw it in the platform's own
   list; otherwise say "the reason that matches: private information /
   harassment / impersonation / child safety".
3. Give the points to make, not a text: the kind of violation, who is
   targeted in general terms (a private person, a child), what the account
   does. No names, no leaked data, no reporter details.
4. Ask for one report per member, from their own account, in their own
   words; say that repeating a report helps nothing.
5. Tell the affected person to file the platform's own first-person
   privacy route if one exists; if you do not know, say that platforms
   often act only on the affected person's own report.
6. Never promise removal; say the platform decides against its own rules
   and may not report the outcome.
7. Child sexual exploitation: never. If the review suggests it, the report
   is routed, not approved (the runbook's section on the routing card).
8. For doxxing: account link by default; the post URL only by your
   recorded opt-in; never the post for intimate images.
9. Add "do not download, save, forward or engage with the content".

## Adding a reference page for a new platform

When a platform recurs, give it a page named
`platform-reporting-<name>.md` in this folder, type `reference`, with the
same section order as the five existing pages: How to report (the in-app
path, then child sexual exploitation, private information, bullying and
harassment, impersonation); Policy pages; Posture on volume, duplicates
and false reports; What the bot's instructions say; Unverified. Rules for
the content:

- Every URL carries a tag from the legend above, or an explicit "verified
  <date> from <URL>" note. Quote policy text verbatim; never upgrade a [W],
  [S] or [INF] claim to a fact.
- Record the platform's own statement on report counts and on false or
  duplicate reports, and whether privacy reports must come from the
  affected person.
- List at the end what could not be verified.
- Regenerate the indexes (`node scripts/okf/build-index.mjs`) and run the
  gate (`node scripts/okf/check.mjs`).
