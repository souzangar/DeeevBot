# Trusted-flagger and NGO partner programmes

Roadmap item (decision Q7 of 2026-10-28): applications to Meta's Trusted
Partner programme and TikTok's Community Partner Channel go on the roadmap;
no public application route was found for either. This page records what
was found on 2026-10-28, so a later attempt starts from facts. Every URL
was fetched or rendered on that date unless marked otherwise.

How to read the tags: [H] raw HTTP fetch, text read verbatim; [R] public
page rendered in a headless browser without login, text read verbatim;
[P] PDF converted to text; [S] seen only in a search result, not read;
[INF] the researcher's inference.

## Why it matters

Documented priority exists only for designated channels. No platform read
documents that report volume raises priority; the measured difference
between an ordinary notice and a trusted-flagger notice is the only
documented speed-up (Instagram: median time to act 29.0 hours for Article
16 notices against 10.3 hours for trusted-flagger notices; Facebook: 9.2
against 8.4 hours; Meta DSA reports of August 2025 [P]).

## What this means for the project

- The only programme with a documented application route is the EU DSA
  trusted-flagger status, and the Commission says "Only EU-based entities
  can apply". The NGO is in Iran. [INF] That route is closed unless an
  EU-based partner organisation applies and relays; nothing read describes
  such relaying.
- Meta and TikTok document their partner channels but not how to join
  them; X documents a prior-consent route for automated NGO reporting but
  not how to request it. These are roadmap items: find the contact
  (through a peer NGO, a conference, or a platform safety team), apply,
  and record the outcome here.
- Critical coverage of Meta's Trusted Partner channel (Rest of World, 2023:
  https://restofworld.org/2023/exporter-meta-trusted-partner-program-slow-decline/;
  an Internews report, https://internews.org/?p=22490) was seen in search
  results only [S]; a second Internews URL returned 404. Read before
  investing effort.
- Until then, the bot's broadcasts rest on what every platform allows:
  genuine, individually filed, substantiated reports. Nothing on this page
  changes the per-platform instructions.
- The owner's statement stands as the starting point: "we don't have
  official way to contact with the platforms and our government don't care
  about these things."

## Related DSA facts

From the EUR-Lex text [R]: Article 16(2) lists what a notice should
contain ("a sufficiently substantiated explanation of the reasons", "the
exact URL or URLs", the submitter's name and email "except in the case of
information considered to involve one of the offences referred to in
Articles 3 to 7 of Directive 2011/93/EU", and "a statement confirming the
bona fide belief" that the notice is accurate and complete); Article 23(2)
lets platforms suspend, "after having issued a prior warning", the
processing of notices from "complainants that frequently submit notices or
complaints that are manifestly unfounded"; Article 23(3) lists what they
weigh ("the absolute numbers ... submitted within a given time frame", "the
relative proportion thereof", "the gravity of the misuses" and "the
intention"). Measured suspensions in the platforms' reports: Meta "We do not
have any volumes to report for this reporting period"; X "Total number |
0"; TikTok "did not suspend the processing of illegal content reports or
complaints due to individuals frequently submitting manifestly unfounded
notices".
