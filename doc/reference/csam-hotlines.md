# CSAM hotlines and report-once guidance

This is the table critical invariant 7 points to: a report the admin
categorises as suspected child sexual abuse material is routed only to the
platform's own report channel and the hotlines listed here, through the
admin's routing card, and is never sent to the user base or to any chat
other than the admin review venue. The procedure around the card is in the
[admin review runbook](../guides/admin-review-runbook.md); the decision is
recorded in
[planning-decisions-2026-10-28.md](../product/planning-decisions-2026-10-28.md)
(Q1). Everything below was compiled on 2026-10-28 from the research note
of that date; every URL was fetched or rendered on that date unless marked
otherwise. The NGO is not a hotline and has no counsel; nothing here is
legal advice.

How to read the tags: [H] raw HTTP fetch, text read verbatim; [R] public
page rendered in a headless browser without login, text read verbatim;
[W] read only through a summarising fetch tool, needs confirmation; [P] PDF
converted to text; [S] seen only in a search result, not read; [INF] the
researcher's inference, not an organisation's statement.

## The rule in one paragraph

Never open, download, save, screenshot, forward or share suspected
material or its link outside the official reporting channels below.
Report the bare URL, once, to the platform and once to one hotline; attach
no files. State in the hotline report that it is an unverified member
report and that the content was not opened. Then let the bot delete the
URL and comment. INHOPE's words: "Do not download, save, or share the
content, as that can be illegal." and "never share links or files with
anyone outside the official reporting channels. Sharing is illegal and can
further harm the child."

## What each organisation says about reporting once, files and sharing

INHOPE [R] https://www.inhope.org/what-we-do/how-to-report:

- "Never send CSAM files via email. The INHOPE Secretariat is not a hotline
  and cannot view, store, or process reports."
- "Reports sent directly to INHOPE cannot be acted on. Only national
  hotlines are trained and legally authorised to handle this content.
  Please submit your report to your national hotline once, using their
  reporting form."
- "Do not share the content with anyone else, as sharing can harm the child
  involved and is illegal."
- "Should I attach files when submitting a report? No. Please only submit
  the URL of the content you want to report never send files as
  attachments."
- "Can I report content via email? No, never share links or files with
  anyone outside the official reporting channels. Sharing is illegal and
  can further harm the child."
- "Should I report the same URL several times? No. Please submit each
  report only once." The research note's cross-platform table quotes the
  fuller sentence: "Please submit each report only once. Sending the same
  URL multiple times does not speed up the process and can slow down our
  hotlines' ability to respond effectively." Reporting is anonymous.

ICMEC and IWF (portal release, [R], above):

- "Do provide the exact URL where child sexual abuse images are located."
- "Do report only once for each web address or URL. Repeat reporting of
  the same URL isn't needed and takes up analysts' time."

IWF [R]:

- The report landing page says "Reporting is quick, easy and anonymous."
- On possession, from a news item of Wed 20 Nov 2019
  (https://www.iwf.org.uk/news-media/news/iwf-warns-possessing-indecent-images-of-children-is-always-a-crime-after-senior-police-officer-is-convicted):
  "No matter who you are, possessing indecent images of children is a
  criminal offence." The case: a video that was "passed ... on to a group
  including Williams in the hopes of alerting her to the abuse and
  prompting action". That is the exact behaviour this project forbids:
  forwarding to alert someone is still distribution. No explicit "do not
  download / forward" sentence was found on the IWF report landing page
  itself.

NCMEC CyberTipline [R] https://report.cybertip.org/faqs:

- "Do I have to live in the United States to make a report? No, anyone can
  make a report of child sexual exploitation or abuse. If possible, please
  include information about where the incident happened and where you are
  located so we can make sure the information is made available to the
  right law enforcement agency."
- Screenshots: "No, you cannot attach them to the CyberTipline report you
  are making but it is important to make a note in the report that you do
  have them." (the admin will have none; the content is never opened).
- No "do not download / forward" sentence was found on the NCMEC pages
  read; the platforms say it (Instagram: "Never download or save this
  content for any reason."; X: "do not like, reply to, quote-post,
  screenshot, follow accounts who distribute, or otherwise engage with the
  content"; TikTok: "Never download, record, or share this kind of
  content.").

Telegram [H] https://telegram.org/safety: Telegram "processes thousands of
reports on CSAM from global third-party organizations via automated
takedown addresses:" abuse@telegram.org and stopCA@telegram.org; "In 2024,
we expanded this database with hashes from organizations like the Internet
Watch Foundation."; the IWF "will report directly into Telegram when child
sexual abuse imagery is detected" (IWF news, 4 Dec 2024, on the Telegram
page).

## How the routing card uses this table

From decision Q1 of 2026-10-28 (procedure in the runbook):

1. The admin categorises from the reporter's description and the context
   visible without opening media; the reporter's label is a hint. Doubt
   about the sexual nature routes; doubt about whether a minor appears does
   not by itself (a child's public photo with insults is child safety or
   harassment).
2. The card carries the URL, and the account's link when it differs, as
   non-tappable code text, the platform's own path (for a Telegram-hosted
   target naming stopCA@telegram.org, as code text), and the links of
   NCMEC, the ICMEC-IWF portal, the IWF portal and the INHOPE directory.
   The admin files one report through the platform's path, one report to
   one hotline (NCMEC or the ICMEC-IWF portal by default, since both take
   reports from anywhere; the IWF portal; or the national hotline found in
   the INHOPE directory where one exists), and for a Telegram-hosted target
   an email to stopCA@telegram.org. The hotline report states "unverified
   member report, content not opened".
3. On confirmation the bot records the admin's id and the timestamp,
   deletes the URL, the account's link, the comment and the
   affected-person flag at once, on the report and on every report merged
   into it (the aggregate fields and the keyed link hash remain), and
   deletes the card from the venue (edits it to the outcome without links
   when it is older than Telegram's 48 hours). It sends the reporter
   nothing. Q1 as first decided had the bot send the reporter a neutral
   acknowledgement with the public hotline links at routing; the scope
   reduction of the same evening removed every decision message, so a
   reporter whose label was "sexual content involving a minor" gets the
   hotline links at intake instead, right after the acknowledgement, and
   can also report directly.
4. Why the link is never broadcast (recorded for the owner): platforms
   deduplicate, so volume buys no speed; every member who opens the link
   accesses the material, a crime for them almost everywhere and a harm; a
   bot that bulk-sends such links distributes access and will be banned by
   Telegram, taking the NGO's channel with it; the hotlines hold direct
   escalation lines the NGO cannot get.

## The Iranian context

- The owner, verbatim: "we don't have official way to contact with the
  platforms and our government don't care about these things." There is no
  state channel in this procedure.
- The research did not check the INHOPE directory's entry for Iran (the
  researcher did not know the NGO's country); whether a national hotline
  exists there is unverified. The default routes are therefore the portals
  that accept reports from anywhere: NCMEC's CyberTipline and the ICMEC-IWF
  portal.
- NCMEC asks for the reporter's location "if possible"; NCMEC and the IWF
  take anonymous reports (names and contact details optional). What an
  admin discloses is their own decision, with the personal-risk notes in
  the runbook in mind.

## Becoming a hotline (not planned)

For the record, if the NGO ever wanted its own channel. INHOPE [R]
https://www.inhope.org/membership/establish-a-hotline: "A hotline needs
legal authority to operate (typically through a Memorandum of Understanding
with law enforcement), trained analysts, secure technology for processing
reports (such as Report Box and ICCAM), a web-reporting form for public
reports, and partnerships with hosting providers for content removal."
Typical timeline: "12 to 22 months from initial contact to provisional
membership". Given the owner's statement about the government, this is not
a route for this NGO.
